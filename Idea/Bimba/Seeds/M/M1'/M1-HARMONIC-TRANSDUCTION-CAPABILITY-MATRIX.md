This companion exists because the current M1 deep capability matrix still preserves an older musical reading in which `A/B/C/D1/D2/D3` appear as six coequal pairing families. The current reviewed derivation is sharper:


Until the long-form M1 matrix is safely folded, this document is authoritative for that harmonic distinction. The generative authority of the core musical theory remains the QL kernel/source programme in `QL-MEF#31/#78`; M1/M1′ consumes, develops and renders that prior formal grammar in Paramaśiva.

The reason for the distinction is operational. `A/B/C` answers **which positional relation is active**. `D1/D2/D3` answers **how completely its direct/conjugate field is being held**. Treating them as one six-item list loses the relation between harmonic selection and conjugate completion.

| Family | Positional operation | What it enables |

## 3. D1 → D2 → D3 conjugate completion

For a selected direct relation `X–Y`, conjugacy opens the positional square `{X,Y,X′,Y′}`. `D1/D2/D3` names the degree to which that square has been made operative:

```text
D1  selected direct relation
    {X,Y}
    2 coordinates

D2  one-sided conjugate expansion
    {X,Y,X′} or {X,Y,Y′}
    3 coordinates

D3  complete conjugate square
    {X,Y,X′,Y′}
    4 coordinates
```

The dynamic grammar is therefore:

```text
position
  → relation A/B/C
  → complement where implicated by the selected family
  → conjugacy
  → D1/D2/D3 degree of completion
  → basis-specific interval realisation
  → lens/tonic anchoring
  → Context-Frame musical function
```

Operator names such as transform / require / complete can describe operations available during the D progression; they do not turn D1/D2/D3 into three additional base pairing families.

## 5. Jankó keyboard as playable Figure

### 5.1 Historical/instrumental fact

The classical Jankó keyboard is an isomorphic six-row keyboard constructed as three repeated touch-points over two interleaved whole-tone row families. In the familiar classical colouring, one row-family presents a `4 white : 2 black` distribution while the alternating row-family presents `3 white : 3 black`; semitone adjacency is diagonal between the whole-tone rows.

This is an **orienting instrumental Figure**, not source authority for QL. Historical keyboard construction establishes the instrument fact; QL source/derivation remains responsible for QL claims.

### 5.2 QL instrumental reading

The Figure is unusually exact for the current M1 musical field because one six-row playable surface can expose both partitions implicated by the Spanda derivation:

```text
sixfold playable surface
      ├─ row-family reading: 3 : 3
      └─ colour/row-family reading: 4 : 2

whole-tone horizontal relation
      ↕ diagonal semitone relation
12-position chromatic completion
```

The project-level proposition is therefore:

> **The Jankó surface is a historically instantiated playable geometry in which the 3:3 and 4:2 partitions can be apprehended together as two organisations of one sixfold/chromatic field.**

This proposition does not claim Jankó derived QL or validates the Spanda theory. It gives M1′ a concrete instrument morphology through which the already-derived QL relations may be played and inspected.

### 5.3 M1′ consequence

A Jankó-like/isomorphic mode of the playable matheme should preserve:

- six visible/tactile rows as one field rather than a decorative piano skin;
- horizontal whole-tone continuity and diagonal semitone adjacency;
- repeated pitch access across rows;
- simultaneous inspection of the `3:3` and `4:2` partitions;
- A/B/C relation highlighting independent of D completion state;
- D1→D3 conjugate expansion visible across direct/prime faces;
- chromatic and fifths traversals as two bases of the same twelve-position substrate;
- exact rational/provenance readout for every sounded relation.

The morphology can therefore become an executable Figure inside `epi.deep.m1`, while the kernel remains the generative authority.
