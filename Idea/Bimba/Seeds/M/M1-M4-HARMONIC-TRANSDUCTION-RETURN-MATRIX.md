## 3. Jankó keyboard Figure — embodied 3:3 / 4:2 geometry

The Jankó keyboard is retained here as a **historical instrumental Figure** for the musical geometry; it is not derivational authority for QL.

Historical keyboard facts:

- Paul von Jankó's original arrangement uses six rows, formed by two interleaved whole-tone manuals repeated as three touch-points for each sounding note.
- Moving along one row advances by whole tone; the neighbouring interleaved row supplies the semitone-shifted complementary whole-tone collection.
- In the traditional piano-derived colouring, one alternating row-family presents **four white and two black keys** per six-note whole-tone cycle, while the other presents **three white and three black**.

This produces a striking physical co-presence of the two native QL partitions:

```text
First Spanda   3:3
               ↕
Jankó row-family with 3 white : 3 black

Second Spanda  4:2
               ↕
Jankó row-family with 4 white : 2 black
```

The QL reading is a project interpretation of that instrument geometry. The historical fact is simply the six-row isomorphic whole-tone construction and its traditional colour distribution.

The reason the Figure matters is operational rather than decorative:

```text
same sixfold surface
   ├─ horizontal whole-tone continuity
   ├─ diagonal semitone relation between interleaved rows
   ├─ repeated touch-points preserve transpositional shape
   └─ 3:3 and 4:2 colour partitions coexist on the playable field
```

This makes the Jankó layout a useful candidate M1′ controller/projection for the QL musical substrate. A future controller mapping should preserve the actual Jankó isomorphism while allowing overlays for:

- direct / prime phase;
- A/B/C relation family;
- D1/D2/D3 conjugate-completion degree;
- chromatic whole-tone helices;
- fifth-cycle address;
- L/L′ tonic/lens anchor;
- active Ananda/Spanda state.

No claim is made here that Jankó historically encoded QL. The relation is that an independently developed musical instrument supplies a concrete playable geometry in which QL's `3:3`, `4:2`, two whole-tone helices and semitone cross-relation can be expressed without forcing the standard piano's asymmetric one-row topology.
