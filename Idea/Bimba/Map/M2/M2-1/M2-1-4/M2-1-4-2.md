# M2-1-4-2 · Epistemology (how/knowing)

> Methodological knowing - establishing how we know

## Detail
- **Description:** Epistemology is Husserl's phenomenological reduction operating - suspending belief in transcendent existence to study immanent givenness. This is where psychology can become phenomenological psychology - studying not facts about psyche but how psyche constitutes its world. The second stage where consciousness becomes reflexive - consciousness examining consciousness. Still relatively formal temporality - logical sequence more than lived history. Husserl's genetic phenomenology begins here - how does consciousness constitute temporal objects?
- **Operational essence:** Operates as second explicate epistemic position - methodological investigation of knowing processes, introducing diachronic dimension. First position in (2-3) dyadic core.
- **Operational symbolics:** Timeline (diachronic axis); microscope (examining process); steps (sequential method); flow chart (process mapping); question mark examining itself
- **Internal structure:** Organized as methodological investigation: Epistemology examines HOW knowing occurs - the processes, conditions, methods. Introduces temporal sequence (first this, then that) but still relatively abstract - not yet fully historical. The diachronic axis emerges: change over time, development, process. But this is formal diachrony - logical/methodological sequence rather than historical becoming.
- **Consciousness structure:** Reflexive methodological consciousness; awareness examining how it knows; the diachronic investigation of process
- **Key principles:** Second-Order Knowing: Reflecting on knowing itself; Phenomenological Epoché: Bracketing to study givenness; Critical Turn: Kant's examination of knowledge conditions; Diachronic Introduction: Process and sequence over time; Methodological Focus: How (process) vs. what (content); Reflexive Consciousness: Knowing examining knowing; Genetic Phenomenology: How consciousness constitutes temporal objects
- **Architectural function:** Second explicate position - the methodological turn where knowing becomes self-examining. Epistemology asks "How do we know?" - not content but process. This is critical knowing - recognizing knowledge is always perspectival, conditioned, constructed rather than simply given. The shift from naive realism to critical awareness. Temporal structure: diachronic (over time) - methodology implies before/after, steps, sequences.
- **Practical applications:** Methodological Analysis: Examining how we come to know; Epoché Practice: Bracketing assumptions to study phenomena; Critical Reflection: Questioning knowledge conditions; Diachronic Study: Tracing development and change; Genetic Investigation: How structures emerge over time; Process Mapping: Identifying steps in knowing; Meta-Cognitive Awareness: Examining our own cognitive processes
- **Resonances:** Husserl: Phenomenological epoché, genetic phenomenology; Kant: Critical philosophy examining possibility of knowledge; Descartes: Methodological doubt as epistemic procedure; Saussure: Diachronic linguistics studying change over time; Genetic Epistemology: Piaget's developmental stages; Process Philosophy: Emphasis on becoming over being; Archetypal Two: "How" questions investigating method
