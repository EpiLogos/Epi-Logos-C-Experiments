## §0/1 — Music as the Audible Face of the Matheme

The fourth file returns the Binary Explication to the full sign with which it began. File 1 established the 0 as the indeterminate fact of aware-being, File 2 followed the / as the living passage of relation, and File 3 unfolded the 1 as the determinate articulation in which relation can be held, read, and brought to expression. We now return to 0/1 because the inquiry has reached the register in which the relation can be sounded. The slash has been ratio all along, and ratio, once it is given vibration, duration, recurrence, and return, becomes interval. Music is the proof heard in the medium where proportion becomes immediately sensible.

This matters because QL has always asked us to hold together two demands that usually pull apart. On one side, the matheme must remain formally accountable: every mark has to be derived, every count has to be earned, every symmetry has to answer to the structure that produced it. On the other side, the matheme began from lived awareness, from the question of how I am present to what I experience, and so its final adequacy cannot lie in a silent diagram alone. A complete derivation must be thinkable, computable, speakable, playable, and observable. Music gives us the first medium in which these powers meet without forcing one of them to disappear into another.

The derivation in this file therefore works in three registers at once. Pure ratio-space gives us exact harmonic relations such as 1/1, 9/8, 4/3, 3/2, 16/9, and 2/1. Twelve-tone pitch-class space gives us cyclic closure, lens anchorings, whole-tone helices, fifth lines, and the operational field of $\mathbb{Z}_{12}$. QL/MEF epistemic space gives us positions, bimba and pratibimba, the six harmonic-pairing families, the seven context-frames, and the 84 mode-tonic landscape. Each register carries a different burden. Ratio gives the arithmetic body, pitch-class space gives the playable topology, and QL gives the epistemic reason why a scale or mode is a way of knowing.

The Sanskrit pair **bimba** and **pratibimba** will carry the two faces of the musical field. Bimba means the original image or standing image; in this derivation it names the canonical face of the substrate, the 0/1 reading, the side from which the field is first held as itself. Pratibimba means reflected image; here it names the conjugate face, the 1/0 reading, the same substrate taken through reflection, recognition, and return. The two terms matter because QL music is built from one field read through two conjugate faces. The musical work of the file is to show how that conjugacy becomes ratio, interval, helix, mode, observer, and instrument.

The derivation has one governing discipline. We begin from the matheme's own ratios, then watch how the ratios generate a chromatic substrate, how the substrate admits two foundational traversals, how those traversals support six families of relation, how the families yield both chromatic and diatonic architectures, and how the resulting scales become epistemic through lens and mode. If the argument has done its work, the musical theory can feel inevitable in the right sense: the relation 0/1, when heard as ratio, has no other way to become fully itself.

## §0 — The Foundational Ratios

The musical derivation begins from the standing identity:

$$
\frac{0}{1} + \frac{1}{0} = \frac{1}{1} \equiv 100\%
$$

This expression has to be read carefully. As ordinary arithmetic, $1/0$ is undefined, and the expression would be illegitimate. In the matheme, however, 0/1 and 1/0 mark the two directional readings of the relation: the void in ratio to unity, and unity in ratio to the void. The first reading collapses toward zero; the second opens toward divergence. Their superposition works as a standing-identity claim across registers: the field is whole because the relation can be traversed in both directions, and because each traversal gives the other its meaning.

When the whole is written as 100%, an internal decomposition becomes available:

$$
100\% = 64\% + 36\%
$$

The proportion between these two portions reduces to:

$$
\frac{64}{36} = \frac{16}{9} = \left(\frac{4}{3}\right)^2
$$

This is the first strictly musical threshold. The quaternary and the hexadic have become a ratio, and that ratio is the doubled perfect fourth. The proof reaches music because the internal decomposition of the standing identity has ratio-form, and ratio-form is already harmonic. The $16/9$ is also one whole-tone short of the octave:

$$
\frac{16}{9} \times \frac{9}{8} = \frac{2}{1}
$$

or, stated as the residue between the octave and the totality ratio:

$$
\frac{2/1}{16/9} = \frac{9}{8}
$$

The epogdoon, $9/8$, is the exact interval by which the QL totality-ratio falls short of octave closure. The system reaches $16/9$, almost doubles, and remains open by precisely one whole-tone. That incompleteness is the motor of the music. A closed octave would give return without residue. The matheme gives a return that requires one more tick, and that tick is the first audible form of process.

Four ratios carry the harmonic skeleton:

| Ratio | Musical name | QL function |
|---|---|---|
| $4/3$ | Perfect fourth | Manifestation and recognition span |
| $3/2$ | Perfect fifth | Completion, lure, harmonic generator |
| $9/8$ | Epogdoon / whole-tone | Tick quantum, the residue of open process |
| $16/9$ | Doubled fourth / Pythagorean minor seventh | Totality one epogdoon short of octave |

The perfect fourth arises because $(4/3)^2 = 16/9$. It is the square-root interval of QL totality, the span that must be applied twice to reach the system's totality-ratio. The perfect fifth arises as the fourth's octave complement:

$$
\frac{4}{3} \times \frac{3}{2} = \frac{2}{1}
$$

The epogdoon arises from the relation between the fifth and the fourth:

$$
\frac{3/2}{4/3} = \frac{9}{8}
$$

This sequence gives us a clean derivational order. The fourth articulates the internal span of totality; the fifth completes that span into octave relation; the whole-tone appears as the difference between them; and $16/9$ names the open totality whose lack of closure requires the whole-tone tick. Music enters because relation has become proportion, and proportion has disclosed a remainder that can only be lived as movement.

## §1 — Two Chromatic Generators

The foundational ratios give two natural ways to traverse a twelvefold pitch-class field. The first uses the epogdoon, $9/8$, as generator. In twelve-tone equal temperament this is the whole-tone step, two semitones. The second uses the perfect fifth, $3/2$, as generator. In twelve-tone equal temperament this is seven semitones. These two traversals are both native to the derivation because each comes from a foundational ratio already earned in §0. The whole-tone gives the system's tick-by-tick advance; the fifth gives the harmonic architecture by which the octave field is generated.

### The Whole-Tone Basis

Begin at C and move by whole-tones. We obtain:

$$
C,\ D,\ E,\ F\sharp,\ G\sharp,\ A\sharp
$$

In pitch-class arithmetic:

$$
WT_0 = \{0,2,4,6,8,10\}
$$

This is the bimba helix. If we begin one semitone higher, at C sharp, and again move by whole-tones, we obtain:

$$
C\sharp,\ D\sharp,\ F,\ G,\ A,\ B
$$

or:

$$
WT_1 = \{1,3,5,7,9,11\}
$$

This is the pratibimba helix. The two helices together exhaust $\mathbb{Z}_{12}$:

$$
WT_0 \cup WT_1 = \mathbb{Z}_{12}
$$

The cross-helix relation in this basis is the semitone. The smallest possible pitch-class difference is the point at which bimba and pratibimba touch. That gives the whole-tone basis its character: it makes the slash audible as the tightest possible cross-over, the minimum difference through which the reflected face appears.

At Lens 0, the position mapping is:

| Position | Bimba note | Position | Pratibimba note |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| 0 | C | 0' | C# |
| 1 | D | 1' | D# |
| 2 | E | 2' | F |
| 3 | F# | 3' | G |
| 4 | G# | 4' | A |
| 5 | A# | 5' | B |

The fifth bimba position, A sharp, is especially important. In pure whole-tone stacking, five epogdoons above C gives:

$$
\left(\frac{9}{8}\right)^5 = \frac{59049}{32768}
$$

This lies close to the QL totality-ratio $16/9$, while remaining distinct from it. The difference between them is the Pythagorean comma. The system will return to this site later because it is where pure ratio-space and equal-tempered closure begin to disagree, and that disagreement is musically the same kind of fruitful residue that the matheme names as aletheic evolution.

### The Fifths Basis

The second chromatic derivation begins from the perfect fifth. In $\mathbb{Z}_{12}$, a fifth is $+7$ semitones, and because $\gcd(7,12)=1$, repeated fifth motion visits every pitch class before returning:

$$
0 \to 7 \to 2 \to 9 \to 4 \to 11 \to 6 \to 1 \to 8 \to 3 \to 10 \to 5 \to 0
$$

In notes from C:

$$
C \to G \to D \to A \to E \to B \to F\sharp \to C\sharp \to G\sharp \to D\sharp \to A\sharp \to F \to C
$$

The first six positions form the bimba fifth-line:

$$
C,\ G,\ D,\ A,\ E,\ B
$$

The next six form the pratibimba fifth-line:

$$
F\sharp,\ C\sharp,\ G\sharp,\ D\sharp,\ A\sharp,\ F
$$

At Lens 0, the fifths-basis position mapping is:

| Position | Bimba note | Position | Pratibimba note |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| 0 | C | 0' | F# |
| 1 | G | 1' | C# |
| 2 | D | 2' | G# |
| 3 | A | 3' | D# |
| 4 | E | 4' | A# |
| 5 | B | 5' | F |

Here the cross-helix relation is the tritone. The same bimba/pratibimba crossing that appears as a semitone in the whole-tone basis appears as maximum symmetric opposition in the fifths basis. The apparent tension tells us that the slash is an operation whose interval-character depends on the generator through which the field is read. When we read by the tick, the crossing is minimal; when we read by harmonic architecture, the crossing is maximal and self-inverse.

The two chromatic bases can now be compared directly:

| Aspect | Whole-tone basis | Fifths basis |
|---|---|---|
| Generator | $9/8$ | $3/2$ |
| Semitone step | +2 | +7 |
| What it foregrounds | Tick, adjacency, epogdoon process | Harmonic architecture, tonal generation |
| Bimba at Lens 0 | C D E F# G# A# | C G D A E B |
| Pratibimba at Lens 0 | C# D# F G A B | F# C# G# D# A# F |
| Cross-helix interval | Semitone | Tritone |

Both bases produce the same twelve pitch classes. They differ because they order the field by different powers of the same matheme. The whole-tone basis lets us hear how the system advances; the fifths basis lets us hear how the system harmonically organises itself.

## §2 — The Six Harmonic-Pairing Families

Once the twelvefold field has been generated, the next question is how its positions pair. Pairing is already more than counting because a pair is a relation made audible. To count the twelve notes without asking how they face one another would leave the musical system as a palette. The matheme requires a grammar of relation because QL asks how identity and difference are held across the terms it has generated.

The sixfold position structure admits six harmonic-pairing families. Three operate within a single pass or helix; three operate across the conjugate relation or within the reflected pass. This gives a 3:3 grammar whose self-similarity matters: the positional architecture of the matheme becomes a pairing architecture at the next level.

| Family | Rule | Pairing character |
|---|---|---|
| A | $(0,1),(2,3),(4,5)$ | Natural dyads within a pass |
| B | $(1,2),(3,4),(5,0)$ | Transitions between dyads, including cycle close |
| C | $(0,5),(1,4),(2,3)$ | Converse mirrors, the $X+Y=5$ relation |
| D1 | $(n,n')$ | Same-position cross-helix Spanda |
| D2 | $(n,(n+1)')$, $(n,(n-1)')$, $(n,(5-n)')$ | Cross-position transform, require, complete |
| D3 | A, B, C on primed positions | Pratibimba-internal invariance |

Family A gives the three natural dyads. In the whole-tone basis, these are whole-tones; in the fifths basis, they are fifths. Family B gives the transitions that move between those dyads. The closing pair $(5,0)$ is crucial because it makes the family cyclic: the end of a pass has to return to its origin, and that return already carries the seed of §5→0.

Family C gives the mirror relation $X+Y=5$. This family is the source of the three square-tetrads, which become intelligible once C has been crossed with D1. First we recognise the bimba mirror pairs:

$$
(0,5),\ (1,4),\ (2,3)
$$

Then we take the D1 partners of each term:

$$
(0,0'),\ (5,5'),\quad (1,1'),\ (4,4'),\quad (2,2'),\ (3,3')
$$

The square is therefore:

$$
\text{square} = C \cup D1
$$

At Lens 0, this gives:

| Square | Positions | Whole-tone basis notes | Fifths basis notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Square 1 | $\{0,5,0',5'\}$ | C, A#, C#, B | C, B, F#, F |
| Square 2 | $\{1,4,1',4'\}$ | D, G#, D#, A | G, E, C#, A# |
| Square 3 | $\{2,3,2',3'\}$ | E, F#, F, G | D, A, G#, D# |

This corrects the order of derivation. The squares are structurally vivid because they are the smallest closed tetrads made by combining within-helix mirror and cross-helix conjugacy. A, B, C, D1, D2, and D3 together form the full grammar; the squares are one concentrated construction inside it.

The interval realisations show why the two chromatic bases must be kept together:

| Family | Whole-tone basis | Fifths basis |
|---|---|---|
| A | Three whole-tones | Three perfect fifths |
| B | Two whole-tones plus cycle-close | Two perfect fourths plus cycle-close |
| C | Minor seventh, tritone, whole-tone | Major seventh, minor third, perfect fifth |
| D1 | Semitone at every position | Tritone at every position |
| D2 | Cross-position semitone/minor-third/varied forms | Cross-position tritone/fourth/varied forms |
| D3 | Same as bimba A/B/C on the reflected helix | Same as bimba A/B/C on the reflected line |

The pairing grammar is the point at which QL begins to sound like a music theory in the strong sense. We are no longer only generating notes. We are deriving the ways notes can answer one another according to the matheme's own logic of adjacency, transition, mirror, crossing, transformation, and invariant reflection. The harmonic field is now relationally articulate.

## §3 — Spanda and Slash-Flip

D1 deserves its own treatment because it is the family in which bimba and pratibimba meet at the same position-index. In Kashmir Shaiva terms, this is the Spanda point: the pulse by which the field is neither inertly identical with itself nor broken into two unrelated halves. In musical terms, D1 is where the same QL position is heard through its two conjugate faces.

In the whole-tone basis, D1 gives six semitone crossings:

| Position | D1 pair | Interval |
|---|---|---|
| 0/0' | C - C# | Semitone |
| 1/1' | D - D# | Semitone |
| 2/2' | E - F | Semitone |
| 3/3' | F# - G | Semitone |
| 4/4' | G# - A | Semitone |
| 5/5' | A# - B | Semitone |

The semitone is the interval excluded by either whole-tone helix taken alone. A whole-tone scale has no semitone inside itself. The semitone appears only when we cross the slash. This is why the whole-tone basis gives such a clean image of Spanda: the pulse is the missing intimacy between the two helices, the nearest possible relation that neither side can generate internally.

In the fifths basis, D1 gives six tritone crossings:

| Position | D1 pair | Interval |
|---|---|---|
| 0/0' | C - F# | Tritone |
| 1/1' | G - C# | Tritone |
| 2/2' | D - G# | Tritone |
| 3/3' | A - D# | Tritone |
| 4/4' | E - A# | Tritone |
| 5/5' | B - F | Tritone |

The tritone is self-inverse in $\mathbb{Z}_{12}$ because $12-6=6$. It is the farthest possible symmetric opposition inside the closed pitch-class circle. The same D1 operation therefore appears as minimum crossing in one basis and maximum symmetric opposition in the other. The reason is now visible: whole-tone reading measures the slash by adjacent tick; fifths reading measures it by harmonic generation. The operation is one; the interval-character changes with the basis that bears it.

This gives a sharper language for slash-flip. Slash-flip names the conjugate-reflection operation through which a position is read from the other face of the field. It may sound as semitone, tritone, minor seventh, whole-tone, or another cross-position relation depending on which family and basis are in play. What remains invariant is the structural act: a term is taken through the relation that makes it itself by showing it otherwise.

The C-family supplies the within-helix complements to D1. In the whole-tone basis:

| C mirror | Notes | Interval | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| $(0,5)$ | C - A# | Minor seventh / $16/9$ region | Totality one tick short of octave |
| $(1,4)$ | D - G# | Tritone | Active mirror, self-inverse axis |
| $(2,3)$ | E - F# | Whole-tone / $9/8$ | Innermost epogdoon mirror |

Read together, C and D1 tell us why the squares are strong musical objects. C supplies the mirror span; D1 supplies the conjugate pulse. The square tetrad is therefore a compact body in which the field remembers both its internal converse relation and its cross-helix reflection. A pianist can strike the tetrad as a chord, but theoretically it is a small topology: the mirror and the slash sounding in one hand.

## §4 — Bimba/Pratibimba Invariance and Topology

D3 states that the internal grammar of bimba is preserved in pratibimba. Whatever A, B, and C mean on the bimba helix, the same families operate on the primed positions. This matters because reflection would be shallow if it merely produced different notes. The reflected face must preserve the grammar of relation while shifting the orientation from which the field is read. That is exactly what D3 gives.

In the whole-tone basis at Lens 0, the pratibimba helix is:

$$
C\sharp,\ D\sharp,\ F,\ G,\ A,\ B
$$

Its A pairs are:

$$
(C\sharp,D\sharp),\ (F,G),\ (A,B)
$$

Its B pairs are:

$$
(D\sharp,F),\ (G,A),\ (B,C\sharp)
$$

Its C mirrors are:

$$
(C\sharp,B),\ (D\sharp,A),\ (F,G)
$$

The pattern is the same as the bimba side, transposed through conjugacy. In the fifths basis, the same invariance holds across the tritone-separated P-line. This is why the musical field can pass through §5→0 without losing itself. At the end of a bimba cycle, the next face is already prepared to carry the same grammar. The return is enriched because the orientation has changed; it is coherent because the grammar has been preserved.

The topology follows from this. Projection 1, the chromatic substrate, is a pair of sixfold cycles related by conjugate reflection. If we treat the two cycles as independent dimensions, the natural surface is toroidal: one circular motion crossed with another. If we treat the conjugate crossing as an identification that changes orientation, the field takes on a Klein-like character. Topology matters here because it tells us what kinds of return the system permits. A circle returns to its start by repetition. A torus permits two independent loopings. A Klein-like reading permits return through inversion. QL music needs all three intuitions because bimba/pratibimba music is cyclic, double-looped, and reflective at once.

The same thought can be put musically. A whole-tone helix can cycle forever without ever touching the semitone it excludes. The paired helix supplies that excluded contact. A fifth line can circle the chromatic field and return by comma-bearing spiral in pure ratio-space or by exact closure in equal temperament. The field is therefore both closed enough to play and open enough to evolve. This is the musical form of the earlier logical claim: identity holds only through difference, and difference becomes intelligible only because relation keeps the field together.

## §5 — Diatonic CF Grammar and the 84-Fold Field

The chromatic substrate gives twelve pitch classes. The diatonic architecture appears when the same field is read through the inner four of each conjugate half:

$$
QL_8 = I_4 + I_4' = 4 + 4'
$$

The remaining positions form the nodal boundary:

$$
N_4 = B_2 + B_2' = 2 + 2'
$$

So the twelvefold field can be read as:

$$
12 = 8_{\text{explicate}} + 4_{\text{implicate-nodal}}
$$

The eight are the sounded octave-process; the four are the boundary-functions that condition the sounding. The eight are two tetrachords joined by the epogdoon. The four are nodal in the sense that they govern span, hinge, closure, and return.

At Lens 0, the lower tetrachord is:

$$
C = 1,\quad D = \frac{9}{8},\quad E = \frac{81}{64},\quad F = \frac{4}{3}
$$

The upper tetrachord is:

$$
G = \frac{3}{2},\quad A = \frac{27}{16},\quad B = \frac{243}{128},\quad C' = 2
$$

The bridge between F and G is:

$$
\frac{3/2}{4/3} = \frac{9}{8}
$$

So the diatonic octave is carried by the load-bearing equation:

$$
\boxed{\frac{4}{3} \times \frac{9}{8} \times \frac{4}{3} = \frac{2}{1}}
$$

This is the scale-making form of the same ratios derived in §0. A perfect fourth, then the epogdoon bridge, then another perfect fourth: tetrachord, bridge, tetrachord. The major scale appears here because the matheme's 4+4' inner architecture has become audible as a Pythagorean diatonic octave. In C:

$$
C,\ D,\ E,\ F,\ G,\ A,\ B,\ C'
$$

with interval pattern:

$$
\frac{9}{8},\ \frac{9}{8},\ \frac{256}{243},\ \frac{9}{8},\ \frac{9}{8},\ \frac{9}{8},\ \frac{256}{243}
$$

The smaller interval, $256/243$, is the Pythagorean leimma:

$$
\frac{4/3}{(9/8)^2} = \frac{256}{243}
$$

The leimma is the residue inside the fourth after two whole-tones have been placed. It is another version of the same theme: a structure tries to fill itself by equal ticks, and a precise remainder appears. That remainder gives the scale its articulated shape, because a ladder of undifferentiated equal steps would lack the internal tension by which a diatonic form becomes recognisable.

The seven unique notes of the diatonic scale carry the seven context-frames. This is where music becomes explicitly epistemic. A mode is a different CF relation taken as ground. At Lens 0:

| Degree | Note | Ratio | CF | Relational character |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| 1 | C | $1/1$ | CF1 `(00/00)` | Ground, undifferentiated tonal origin |
| 2 | D | $9/8$ | CF2 `(0/1)` | First distinction, standing identity in motion |
| 3 | E | $81/64$ | CF3 `(0/1/2)` | Triadic circulation, tonal desire |
| 4 | F | $4/3$ | CF4 `(0/1/2/3)` | Tetradic closure, lower-tetrachord completion |
| 5 | G | $3/2$ | CF5 `(4.0/1-4.4/5)` | Fractal-doubling executive, harmonic center-crossing |
| 6 | A | $27/16$ | CF6 `(4.5/0)` | Bridge, partial synthesis |
| 7 | B | $243/128$ | CF7 `(5/0)` | Synthesis-return, leading-tone pull |
| 8 | C' | $2/1$ | CF1' | Enriched return |

The fifth degree carries particular weight. G is the perfect fifth, the generator of $\mathbb{Z}_{12}$ under repeated fifth motion, the bridge into the upper tetrachord, and the place where CF5's fractal-doubling character becomes audible. The fifth is consonant because its ratio is simple, and in QL it is also structurally central because it is where the scale's contextual recursion becomes a sound. The ear has long treated the fifth as a privileged relation; this derivation grounds that privilege in the architecture of the matheme.

The twelve lens scales are then obtained by anchoring the same diatonic form at each chromatic position:

D_8(a) = a + \{0,2,4,5,7,9,11,12\}
$$

with:

T_4(a) = a + \{0,2,4,5\},\quad T_4'(a) = a + \{7,9,11,12\}

The lens is the scale-beneath. The CF mode is the way of knowing from within it.

The full scalar field is therefore:

$$
12\ \text{lens scales} \times 7\ \text{CF modes} = 84\ \text{mode-tonic instances}
$$

This is the central epistemic claim of the file. A lens chooses the chromatic anchoring of the scale-beneath. A mode chooses which CF relation becomes the local ground. A full musical perspective therefore requires both coordinates: where the field is anchored, and which form of knowing is taken as home.

The comma-site returns here with greater force. In equal temperament, A sharp and B flat occupy the same pitch class. In pure Pythagorean ratio-space, five whole-tones above C and the $16/9$ totality-ratio differ:

$$
\frac{(9/8)^5}{16/9} = \frac{531441}{524288}
$$

This is the Pythagorean comma, about 23.46 cents. The comma is the difference between a circle that closes by temperament and a spiral that remembers its ratio-history. QL needs this difference because the matheme also returns without merely repeating. Equal temperament gives the playable circle; pure ratio gives the evolutionary residue. The musical system is faithful to both because the matheme itself is a closed-enough instrument with an open-enough history.

## §5→0 — Observer, Performance, Instrument

The derivation has now produced the musical field from the matheme's own resources: the foundational ratios, the two chromatic generators, the six pairing families, the Spanda crossing, the bimba/pratibimba invariance, the diatonic CF grammar, and the 84-fold lens-mode landscape. The final movement asks how such a field becomes an instrument. An instrument is more than a map of notes. It is a body of possible action, a way for a relation to be heard, tested, repeated, varied, and known from inside the act of performance.

The observer layer enters after generation because the system already knows how to produce its tones. FFT, CQT, chromagram, and cymatic rendering let the system listen back to its own sounding.

A chromagram folds sounding energy into the twelve pitch classes:

$$
p_k = \mathrm{round}\left(12\log_2\left(\frac{f_k}{f_{\mathrm{ref}}}\right)\right) \bmod 12
$$

$$
C_p(t) = \sum_{k:p_k=p}|X(k,t)|^2
$$

The result is a twelve-dimensional pitch-class vector that can be read through the active lens. The generated sound can now be compared with the intended QL state. If a performance is moving through Lens 0 Mixolydian, for example, the observer can tell whether the sounded field really emphasises the CF5 ground, whether the off-scale pitch classes are operating as passing tensions, whether the square-tetrad being struck is pulling the chromagram into the expected family relation, and whether the performed cadence is returning as §5→0 or merely landing on a familiar tonic.

The cymatic layer makes the 8+4 distinction visible. The eight explicate positions are sung, struck, or synthesised as active tones. The four nodal positions condition the boundary of the standing-wave form. In a Chladni-style image, particles gather along nodal lines because those regions remain relatively still while the surface vibrates around them. That gives a precise image for the QL partition: the audible eight supply motion; the implicate four supply boundary, hinge, and contour. Sound becomes visible because the field is held by an unsounded boundary.
